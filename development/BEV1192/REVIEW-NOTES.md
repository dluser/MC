# SME review notes: Draught System Components guided widgets (v5)

All learner-facing content comes from the "Draught System Components" handout (slides and speaker notes). Please confirm the items below.

## Corrections to confirm
1. **Nitrogen fitting is CGA 580, not CGA 380** (Regulators). The handout notes say 380. Supplier and manufacturer listings for nitrogen beverage regulators specify CGA 580 (shared with argon and helium).
2. **Pints per keg** (Kegs). The widget calculates gallons × 128 ÷ 16, which gives ≈124 pints for a ½ bbl. The handout says "about 120."
3. **Gas-line color codes** (Tubing). The handout calls beer gas 30/70 CO₂/N₂, but lists "red for 70/30." Please confirm the ratio order for red.

## New illustrative details in v3 (please confirm)
- **Faucets: standard vs. ventless look.** The standard faucet is drawn with small vent holes on its body; the ventless faucet has none and a longer, smooth, tapered spout. This visual cue isn't in the handout. Please confirm it matches the faucets your students will see.
- **Kegs: cooler display reads 38°F** in the foamy-keg activity. The handout doesn't give a temperature. 38°F is a common draught cooler setting; change or remove it if you prefer.
- **Kegs: keg valve cross-section.** It shows a simplified Sankey valve: an outer CO₂ valve ring, a central ball valve on a spring, the spear (down tube), and the seal between the two valves, chipped, with gas leaking into the beer path. Please check the anatomy and labels.
- **Couplers: Thomas valve and beer washer.** The Thomas valve is drawn on the coupler's gas inlet as a ball-and-spring check valve (gas passes toward the keg, beer pushing back is blocked). The beer washer is a rubber ring between the coupler outlet and the beer tailpiece (tailpiece, nut, washer, coupler). Please confirm the placement and internals.
- **Couplers and Faucets: corrosion cross-section.** Solid stainless steel next to chrome-plated brass with a chip, a corrosion pit growing into the brass, and microbes in the pit. It's a concept diagram, not to scale.
- **Safety: symptoms shown on a worker.** The handout's symptom list is grouped into five poses: 2–3% short of breath; 5% heavy breathing and sweating; 7.5% hand to head, dizzy; 10% kneeling, nauseous; 30% collapsed. Short symptom tags use the handout's words in shortened form (for example "Losing consciousness," "Racing heart" for increased heart rate).
- **Safety: storage floor plan.** Four areas: cool storeroom, warm back room (radiator, sunny window), boiler room and a delivery van on a hot day. The cylinder starts in the van. Pressure stays qualitative (Normal / Rising / Danger).
- **Safety: heat and pressure animation.** A thermometer rises while the gas molecules inside a cut-away cylinder speed up, the gauge climbs and arrows push on the walls. Qualitative only.
- **Gas: oxidation animation.** A fresh pint turns dull and flat as O₂ gets in, then cardboard, stale bread and vinegar icons stand for the off-flavors named in the handout.
- **Gas: air compressor** in the practice question is a generic shop compressor.
- **Tubing: direct vs. long draw side view.** Direct draw is shown as a kegerator with the keg right under the tower. Long draw is shown as a walk-in cooler with a trunk line through the wall to a distant bar. "Long distance" isn't given as a number.
