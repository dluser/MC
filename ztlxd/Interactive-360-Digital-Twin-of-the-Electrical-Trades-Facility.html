The Challenge:
An electrical trades SME needed a way to address learners who weren't present for in-person demonstrations, without requiring instructors to repeat live demonstrations for every absent student. The SME's initial request was simple photo documentation of the space, but static photos wouldn't capture the spatial and procedural context students needed from hands-on stations.

The Solution:
Rather than delivering flat photo documentation, I proposed and built a 360-degree interactive digital twin of the electrical trades facility — a fully navigable virtual replica of the classroom. Students can move through the space, interact with specific stations and objects, and encounter embedded assessment questions tied to each area, replicating the instructional value of an in-person walkthrough without requiring an instructor to physically repeat it. This gave the SME a scalable, reusable alternative to live re-demonstration and gave students a self-paced way to review station-specific procedures.
